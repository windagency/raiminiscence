# v4 Spreading Activation's hop depth becomes configurable, not fixed at one

[Back to 0020-v13-checkpoint-via-stop-hook.md](0020-v13-checkpoint-via-stop-hook.md) • [Back to 0025-v19-opportunistic-consolidation-debounce.md](0025-v19-opportunistic-consolidation-debounce.md) • [Back to docs/adr/README.md](README.md) • [Back to hld.md](../architecture/hld.md)

[ADR-0006](./0006-spreading-activation-boost-only.md) and [ADR-0008](./0008-v3-spreading-activation-can-surface.md) both kept Spreading Activation — boost and surfacing alike — at exactly one hop, an invariant both ADRs treated as a system-wide guarantee against turning the mechanism into unconstrained, unpredictable candidate injection. v4 replaces that fixed invariant with a configurable `maxHops` (a project-level `.raiminiscence.config.json` setting, overridable per `recall` call; unset defaults to `1`, i.e. every prior version's exact behaviour, and hard-capped at 3 regardless of configuration), and lets both boost and surfacing walk that many hops through non-qualifying intermediate candidates — a qualifying candidate's boost can now draw on an intermediate's own edges to reach a second qualifying candidate it has no direct edge to, and a non-qualifying candidate can surface through a chain of intermediates rather than only a direct edge to a qualifying anchor.

We chose to lift the one-hop ceiling now, rather than leave it in place indefinitely as ADR-0008 anticipated, because the boost-then-surface mechanism has had two full versions of production use with no reported false-positive problem, and because a configurable, opt-in, hard-capped depth preserves every existing safeguard rather than discarding them: the eligibility floor escalates with distance (each hop's weakest edge must clear `4 × hop count`, not just the flat v2/v3 floor), the walk is a bounded visited-set traversal that can't cycle, every intermediate must satisfy the same Scope/`kind` filter a returned result would, and ranking stays strictly monotonic by distance — an independent match always outranks a 1-hop result, which always outranks a 2-hop result, and so on. This is the first user-facing tuning knob (config surface was previously limited to the embedding provider only) and the first mechanism with a genuine multi-step graph walk rather than a single pass over the qualifying pool; both are deliberate departures from v1–v3's own precedent, made because this specific extension is opt-in and bounded rather than because the precedent itself was wrong.

`RecallResultItem.surfacedVia` changes from `"match" | "connection"` to `"match" | "connection-1" | "connection-2" | "connection-3"` — a breaking rename of v3's plain `"connection"` value, accepted because both packages are still pre-1.0 (`0.1.0`) with no compatibility guarantee or migration tooling yet.
